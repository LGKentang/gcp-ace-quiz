# AI tooling reshapes the 2026 ACE blueprint

The Google Cloud Associate Cloud Engineer exam now follows an **unversioned guide weighted ~20% / ~30% / ~30% / ~20%** across four sections: setup, planning and implementation, operations, and access and security. It is the only weighting you should author against ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Compared with the previous "v4.0" guide (~23/30/27/20), the refresh moves three points from setup to operations. It grows the blueprint from **80 to 94 objective bullets**, and the additions cluster in three areas: AI tooling and agents, GPUs and TPUs, and keyless identity and encryption. The new items include Gemini CLI, Google Antigravity, Application Design Center, Agent Runtime, Workbench, Cloud Workstations, Managed Lustre, CMEK, Cloud Hub, and Workforce and Workload Identity Federation ([v4.0 PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/v4.0_associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Third-party sources date the cutover to **June 30, 2026**, so most mock exams and blog cheat sheets written earlier describe a superseded blueprint ([examos.io](https://examos.io/blog/google-ace-exam-june-2026-refresh); [DirectCertify](https://www.directcertify.com/blog/google-cloud-certification-changes-2026-pearson-vue-and-ai-exam-overhaul/)). The format is unchanged: 2 hours, 50–60 multiple-choice and multiple-select questions, $125. Delivery moved to Pearson VUE on March 2, 2026 ([Cert page](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/cloud-engineer); [Google support](https://support.google.com/cloud-certification/answer/16803278?hl=en)). There are almost no first-hand reports from candidates who sat the exam between May and November 2026. Original questions should therefore track the live guide bullet by bullet, use current product names, and draw distractors from products that were retired or renamed in 2025–2026.

## The live PDF's 20/30/30/20 beats every competing weighting

At least five different weightings circulate in 2026, and only one comes from the document Google currently serves. The live standard guide is titled simply "Associate Cloud Engineer Exam Guide | English", with no version number or effective date, and it prints **~20% / ~30% / ~30% / ~20%** ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). The file explicitly titled "v4.0" prints **~23% / ~30% / ~27% / ~20%** and says it "will be live on June 30" without a year ([v4.0 PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/v4.0_associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). That v4.0 is the predecessor and not the current guide is clear from its content. It lacks Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Application Design Center, Agent Runtime, GPU/TPU selection and Managed Lustre, and it still lists "Knative serving" as a compute choice. The live guide contains all of those and uses the "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform" names, which only came into existence at Google Cloud Next '26 on April 22, 2026 ([Agent Platform release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/release-notes?authuser=1); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/google-finally-builds-the-ai-and-agent-platform-its-been-describing-for-years/)). Some 2026 commentary calls the June 2026 guide "v4.0". That label is wrong, and any 23/30/27/20 figure attached to the new content belongs to the old document.

The live PDF wins for three reasons. It is first-party. It is the file linked from the certification page today. And its weights agree with its own content changes: operations gained four new compute bullets, a CMEK bullet, a firewall-management bullet and Cloud Hub, which justifies the larger share. The examos.io figure appears to be a transcription error, since it pairs the new content with weights that appear in no Google document ([examos.io](https://examos.io/blog/google-ace-exam-june-2026-refresh)). CertLand's split still uses the old "Planning and configuring" and "Deploying and implementing" section names ([CertLand](https://certland.net/blog/google-cloud-associate-cloud-engineer-ace-study-guide-2026/)).

**The go-live date is the one remaining uncertainty.** Google prints no date on the live guide. I found no Google announcement of the refresh, and the June 30, 2026 date rests only on third-party sites. The year of v4.0 is also uncertain, but two clues point to 2025. The v4.0 PDF was rendered by an older Chrome PDF engine (m139, around August 2025) than the live file (m151). And the live guide uses names that did not exist before April 2026 ([v4.0 PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/v4.0_associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf); [Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Google also has not said whether the Japanese, Spanish and Portuguese exams moved to the new guide at the same time, and v4.0's note covered only the English exam. Weights are approximate, and Google does not publish per-section question counts or a passing score. The 70% and 75% figures that circulate are vendor estimates ([open-exam-prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/blog/google-associate-cloud-engineer-exam-guide-2026)).

The renewal exam follows a different blueprint. It runs 1 hour with 20 questions for $75, in English and Japanese only, and opens 180 days before expiry ([Cert page](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/cloud-engineer)). It **drops Section 1 entirely, weights Sections 2 and 3 at ~40% each, and limits Section 4 (~20%) to service accounts (4.2)**, omitting IAM objective 4.1 ([Renewal guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_renewal_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Its wording matches the live standard guide, including the "supported by AI tooling" role description, which confirms that both guides were refreshed together. A renewal-track question bank should therefore skip billing and resource-hierarchy setup. Starting in 2026, Google also offers renewal through continuing education in Google Skills, which adds one year of validity ([Google support: renewal](https://support.google.com/cloud-certification/answer/9907853)). ACE's inclusion in that program is confirmed only by a secondary source ([DirectCertify](https://www.directcertify.com/blog/google-cloud-certification-changes-2026-pearson-vue-and-ai-exam-overhaul/)).

## Fourteen net new bullets concentrate on AI, accelerators and keyless identity

Comparing the two official PDFs shows a mostly additive refresh. The role description now says the engineer performs platform tasks "**supported by AI tooling**" ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Gemini Cloud Assist was already in v4.0, for resource analysis with Cloud Asset Inventory and for Cloud Monitoring, so it is not a 2026 addition. Neither are Hyperdisk, AlloyDB, NetApp Volumes, Managed Kafka, Memorystore, Database Center, Managed Prometheus, Personalized Service Health, Ops Agent or Active Assist ([v4.0 PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/v4.0_associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Several vendors label these as "new for 2026", but they were in scope a year earlier. A bank that already covers v4.0 needs only the changes in the table below.

Two of these changes matter most for authoring. First, the **removal of the Terraform versioning and state-management bullet** shifts S2.4 from IaC lifecycle mechanics toward recognizing tools. A question like "which tool fits this need" now matches the blueprint better than one about state-file locking. Second, **identity federation now appears in two sections**. Workforce Identity Federation, for human users from an external identity provider, sits in S1. Workload Identity Federation, for external or GKE workloads, sits in S4. That makes the two an obvious confusable pair. Deployment Manager, Infrastructure Manager and App Engine appear in neither guide ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Delivery changes do not affect content, according to Google. The last Kryterion test date was February 22, 2026, Pearson VUE (OnVUE or test center) began March 2, and the retake policy is unchanged ([Google support](https://support.google.com/cloud-certification/answer/16803278?hl=en)). Since October 29, 2026, CM Connect sign-in requires Google Skills credentials ([Google support: renewal](https://support.google.com/cloud-certification/answer/9907853)). One vendor mentions a strikethrough tool in the new exam interface, but no official source confirms it ([examos.io](https://examos.io/blog/google-ace-exam-june-2026-refresh)).

## Domain-by-domain authoring map with weightings and scenario themes

Google publishes section weights but no sub-objective weights. The suggested shares below are an authoring heuristic. I started from each sub-objective's bullet count in the live guide, then adjusted for how many distinct scenarios each bullet can support. Bullet counts show a structural fact worth acting on. Section 4 carries 20% of the exam with only 11 bullets, about **1.8 percentage points per bullet**. Section 3 carries 30% across 43 bullets, about **0.7 points per bullet**. Sections 1 and 2 sit at 1.25 ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). In practice, each IAM and service-account objective probably gets tested several times, while many operations bullets get a single question each. A bank should go deep on Section 4 and broad on Section 3. The "per 100" column scales directly: a 50-question mock uses roughly 10/15/15/10 by section.

### Section 1: setting up a cloud solution environment (~20%)

| Sub-objective (bullets) | Per 100 | Scenario themes for original questions |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Projects and accounts (12) | 15 | Design a folder hierarchy for business units and apply an org policy to one folder only. A developer in an org created after May 2024 cannot create a service-account key: explain why and give the right fix or alternative. Federate contractors from an external IdP into the console without Cloud Identity accounts (Workforce Identity Federation). Provision Cloud Identity users and groups by hand or automatically. Enable an API before first use. Hit a regional CPU or GPU quota and request an increase. Check that a product or machine type exists in a target region or zone. Inventory resources across the org with Cloud Asset Inventory, then use Gemini Cloud Assist to analyze them. |
| 1.2 Billing (4) | 5 | Link new projects to the right billing account. Set budgets with threshold alerts, and recognize that alerts notify but do not cap spend. Configure billing export to BigQuery for label-based chargeback. Run multiple billing accounts for subsidiaries. |

Budgets alert but do not stop spending, and capping spend requires programmatic action such as a function that disables billing ([CertLand](https://certland.net/blog/google-cloud-associate-cloud-engineer-ace-study-guide-2026/)). The secure-by-default baseline makes good S1 material because it changes the correct answer for classic scenarios. For organizations created on or after May 3, 2024, it blocks service-account key creation and upload, stops automatic Editor grants to default service accounts, enforces domain-restricted sharing, and enforces uniform bucket-level access. Changing any of these requires `roles/orgpolicy.policyAdmin` ([Security baseline constraints](https://docs.cloud.google.com/resource-manager/docs/secure-by-default-organizations?hl=en)).

### Section 2: planning and implementing a cloud solution (~30%)

| Sub-objective (bullets) | Per 100 | Scenario themes for original questions |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 Compute (12) | 13 | Pick the compute option for a stated constraint, with "minimal operational overhead" pointing to Cloud Run or GKE Autopilot. Host a conversational agent on managed infrastructure (Agent Runtime) instead of self-managed GKE. Choose GPU or TPU for a training or inference workload. Run fault-tolerant batch jobs on Spot VMs, and right-size with custom machine types. Choose zonal PD, regional PD or Hyperdisk for a database VM that must survive a zone failure. Centralize SSH access with OS Login, and patch a fleet with VM Manager. Build an autoscaled MIG from an instance template. Deploy GKE as Autopilot, regional or private. Process an object-upload or Pub/Sub event with Cloud Run functions and Eventarc. |
| 2.2 Storage and data (4) | 7 | Choose a database: Cloud SQL, AlloyDB (high-performance PostgreSQL), Spanner (global relational), Bigtable (time series and IoT), Firestore (mobile documents) or BigQuery (analytics). Move existing Kafka clients to Managed Service for Apache Kafka rather than rewriting for Pub/Sub. Choose shared file storage among Filestore, NetApp Volumes and Managed Lustre (HPC and AI training). Write a Cloud NGFW policy rule that targets secure Tags or a service account instead of network tags. Choose Cloud VPN or Cloud Interconnect for hybrid connectivity on private IP ranges. Recognize which AI-assisted tool fits a task: Gemini CLI in a terminal, Gemini Cloud Assist in the console, Application Design Center, or Google Antigravity. |

Mapping "minimal overhead" to Cloud Run or Autopilot and "most cost-effective" to Spot or lifecycle rules is consistent across third-party prep material ([CertLand](https://certland.net/blog/google-cloud-associate-cloud-engineer-ace-study-guide-2026/)). For S2.4's AI tools, the guide names the products but not their capabilities. None of the sources here describes what Antigravity or Application Design Center does at a level suitable for an answer key. Keep these questions at recognition level and verify each tool's purpose against current Google documentation before writing the key. This is the biggest gap for authors in the 2026 blueprint.

The most testable 3.1 fact changed in 2025. Container Registry stopped accepting writes on March 18, 2025, and GKE could no longer read images from it after June 3, 2025. Repositories migrated to Artifact Registry keep their gcr.io URLs ([Container Registry deprecation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/container-registry/docs/deprecations/container-registry-deprecation?authuser=0); [GKE transition doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/deprecations/container-registry); [Transition from Container Registry](https://docs.cloud.google.com/artifact-registry/docs/transition/transition-from-gcr)). Questions that treat "Autopilot vs Standard" as a strict cluster-level choice are therefore dated ([About Autopilot mode in Standard](https://docs.cloud.google.com/kubernetes-engine/docs/concepts/about-autopilot-mode-standard-clusters); [KubeCon EU 2026 blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/gke-and-oss-innovation-at-kubecon-eu-2026)).

## Renamed and retired products supply the best 2026 distractors

The highest-value distractors are answers that were correct two years ago. **Deployment Manager** lost support on April 1, 2026. It keeps running for existing customers only until June 30, 2027, does not accept new organizations, and Google points users to Infrastructure Manager or Terraform ([Deployment Manager deprecations](https://docs.cloud.google.com/deployment-manager/docs/deprecations)). **Cloud Functions** became Cloud Run functions on August 21, 2024 ([Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/serverless/google-cloud-functions-is-now-cloud-run-functions?authuser=4)). **Vertex AI** became Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform: Agent Engine is now Agent Runtime, Vertex AI Studio is Agent Studio, and Vertex AI Search is Agent Search ([Agent Platform release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/release-notes?authuser=1)). `gcloud storage` is the recommended Cloud Storage CLI over `gsutil`, though the two differ in some behavior, such as symlink handling in `rsync` ([gsutil transition](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/gsutil-transition-to-gcloud)). The 2026 exam guide itself uses the new names, so stems and keys should too. Older names can still appear as wrong options, with explanations that call them legacy.

Storage still produces some of the most reliable traps, and one classic fact is now outdated. Minimum storage durations are **30 days for Nearline, 90 for Coldline and 365 for Archive**, and those three classes charge retrieval fees. Every class, including Archive, returns data "within milliseconds, not hours or days", which catches candidates who assume Archive behaves like AWS Glacier. SLAs differ by class and location: Standard is 99.95% in multi- and dual-region and 99.9% in a region, while the colder classes are 99.9% and 99.0% respectively. A new zonal **Rapid** class, with no minimum duration and no retrieval fee, means "Cloud Storage has four storage classes" is no longer strictly true. Enabling Autoclass on an existing bucket moves objects to Standard ([Storage classes doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/storage/docs/storage-classes)). For firewalls, the reliable mechanics are these. Ingress is denied and egress allowed by default. Priorities run 0–65535 with a default of 1000, and the lower number wins. Deny beats allow only at equal priority. Rules are stateful. The four default-network allow rules sit at priority 65534 and do not exist in custom VPCs. Unlike VPC rules, NGFW policy rules cannot share a priority ([VPC firewall rules doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/firewall/docs/firewalls)). Cloud NAT is outbound-only, which makes "use Cloud NAT to expose a private VM" a clean wrong answer ([CertLand](https://certland.net/blog/google-cloud-associate-cloud-engineer-ace-study-guide-2026/)).

The live guide also implies a set of confusable pairs that make natural answer options: Workforce vs Workload Identity Federation; impersonation vs short-lived credentials vs keys; log buckets vs router sinks vs Log Analytics; Cloud Asset Inventory vs Gemini Cloud Assist; Active Assist vs Cloud Hub vs Personalized Service Health; Filestore vs NetApp Volumes vs Managed Lustre; Pub/Sub vs Managed Kafka; and VPC firewall rules vs NGFW policies ([Live guide PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/associate_cloud_engineer_exam_guide_english.pdf)). Several other long-standing ACE traps make good themes but were not re-verified against documentation for this report: the additive nature of IAM inheritance, where custom roles can be created, regional vs zonal MIGs and autohealing, Spot vs legacy preemptible runtime limits, peering's non-transitivity, which audit log types are on by default, and `gcloud config set` vs named configurations. Check each against the current product docs before using it as an answer key.

## Conclusion

The 2026 refresh adds a new kind of question to the ACE more than it adds new facts. The added bullets ask candidates to recognize which AI assistant, agent host or accelerator fits a task, often without the deep configuration knowledge that older ACE questions tested. That leaves original authors with a tension. The blueprint rewards questions on Gemini CLI, Antigravity, Application Design Center and Agent Runtime, but no test-taker has documented how Google actually tests them. The defensible approach is to keep those items at a light recognition level, one or two per 50 questions, and spend the depth budget where the blueprint concentrates its weight: keyless service-account patterns, inherited IAM, and operational changes made without downtime. The most distinctive thing a 2026-accurate bank can do is make the 2024–2026 platform changes part of the answer logic. Secure-by-default organizations, Artifact Registry-only images, the end of Deployment Manager, Autopilot inside Standard clusters and the Rapid storage class all change which answer is correct, and older banks still treat the previous answers as right.

Two facts would most improve this picture: an official Google statement dating the current guide, and a set of first-hand post-July 2026 exam reports. Until those exist, treat the live PDF at services.google.com as the specification. Re-check it before each authoring cycle, because Google changed it without a version number and could do so again.
